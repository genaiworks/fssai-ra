# Residual Risk Register (Third Audit)

Risks are ranked by severity. Status values are:
**accepted** (a deliberate property of the reference implementation),
**open** (a real hardening opportunity not yet addressed here), and
**mitigated-in-fixture-only** (handled inside the offline harness in a way that
does not carry to deployment). Owner roles are the accountable roles named by the
governance model, not individuals. Related-work citations mark where established
prior art limits a claim.

| # | Risk | Why it matters | Status | Deployment obligation / stop condition to close it | Accountable owner role |
|---|------|----------------|--------|----------------------------------------------------|------------------------|
| 1 | **Signing-key custody: reproducible fixture keys vs a real signing service (E1 residual).** | Rule 1 depends on the model being unable to reach the approval private key. The offline harness uses reproducible fixture keys; the default is now random per-instance, but reproducibility is a harness property, not a deployment guarantee. | mitigated-in-fixture-only | In deployment the approval private key must live in a signing service or HSM the model and generated code cannot reach; verify the executor holds only the public verification key. Close only when key custody is externally attested. | Security / key-custody owner |
| 2 | **Compromised trusted mediator or common-mode failure outside the model boundary.** | All guarantees assume an intact executor, disclosure gate, and evidence store. A compromised mediator, or a common-mode fault shared across controls, is outside the model boundary and outside what the offline suite tests. | open (accepted as out of scope for offline proof) | Independent assurance of the trusted base (code review, runtime attestation, isolation from the model). No offline experiment can close this; the paper states no experiment proves immunity to a compromised mediator. | Platform / infrastructure owner |
| 3 | **End-to-end composition on non-education backends (E2 residual).** | The revoke-before-execute recheck (`context_authority_live`) is enforced in the education/action path. Separately passing action and disclosure suites does not by itself prove end-to-end composition on every backend. | open | Add the context-authority recheck and its regression coverage to each backend's execute path before relying on composed action+disclosure guarantees there. | Governance engineering owner |
| 4 | **In-memory pending-outcome / approval-use store (durability).** | `PendingOutcomeStore` and `ApprovalUseStore` are in-memory teaching stand-ins. A crash loses pending outcomes and the single-use record that prevents approval replay. | open | Replace with a durable transactional outbox and a persisted approval-use ledger; see the transactional-outbox pattern (Helland 2012; Richardson). Close when pending outcomes and approval-use survive process restart. | Platform / data-store owner |
| 5 | **Break-glass emergency access accepts an empty justification string.** | Break-glass is bounded, short-lived, and reviewed, but an empty justification weakens the after-the-fact review trail and invites rubber-stamping (automation bias — Skitka 1999; Goddard 2012). | open | Require a non-empty justification at break-glass invocation; a cheap hardening. Close when the empty string is rejected. | Emergency-access reviewer role |
| 6 | **No clock-skew / NTP validation on approval expiry.** | Approval expiry is time-based. Without skew/NTP validation, a wrong clock could accept an expired approval or reject a live one. | open | Validate clock source and bound tolerable skew on expiry checks. Close when skew handling is implemented and tested. | Platform / time-source owner |
| 7 | **Covert channels and inference from rare attributes limit the disclosure-gate and tokenization claims.** | The disclosure gate mediates explicit outputs only. It does not stop covert or side channels — timing, encoded parameters, action choice (Lampson 1973) — and pseudonymizing direct identifiers does not prevent re-identification from quasi-identifiers or rare attributes (Sweeney 2000/2002; Narayanan–Shmatikov 2008). The gate provides access control, not a differential-privacy guarantee (Dwork 2006). | accepted (claims scoped) | Scope privacy claims to explicit flows and access control; do not claim anonymity or covert-channel confinement. Close a stronger claim only with a formal channel analysis and, for anonymity, a k-anonymity/DP mechanism. | Privacy officer role |
| 8 | **"Effectively once," not "exactly once" (distributed delivery).** | Guaranteed exactly-once side effects are impossible over unreliable channels and under asynchronous crash faults (Akkoyunlu 1975; FLP 1985). At-least-once delivery plus idempotent processing yields effectively-once, not exactly-once (Helland 2012). | accepted (claim to be worded as effectively-once) | Word the delivery claim as "effectively once via idempotency keys"; ensure consumers are idempotent. Close the wording gap where the paper/artifacts still say "exactly-once." | Governance engineering owner |
| 9 | **CoT monitoring is an optional, defeatable aid, not a control boundary.** | Chain-of-thought is unfaithful (Turpin 2023; Chen 2025) and monitorability is fragile and can be obfuscated under optimization pressure (Baker 2025; Korbak 2025). Treating CoT as a boundary would place the model back in the trusted base. | accepted | Treat CoT as advisory telemetry outside the trusted computing base; bind approval to the exact proposed action, not the stated reasoning. | Governance engineering owner |
| 10 | **Adaptive / RL and frontier-model attackers NOT RUN.** | The committed adaptive attacker is offline search (static/random/bandit) under a bounded budget. Adaptive and RL attackers break defenses evaluated only against static attacks (Zhan et al. NAACL 2025; Nasr et al. 2025; RL-Hammer 2025); the bandit is below the state of the art. Robustness numbers need adaptive attackers. | open | Evaluate against a genuine RL and a frontier-model attacker; report end-to-end harm prevented, not blocked attempts (WASP). Close when adaptive-attacker numbers exist. | Red-team / evaluation owner |
| 11 | **Novelty is the integration/transfer method, not mediation itself.** | Reference-monitor-plus-IFC for agents is established prior art (Progent; RTBAS; Conseca; f-secure-LLM; and classically Anderson 1972; Denning 1976). The contribution must be stated as the integration and cross-sector transfer method, and differentiated from these, not as new mediation. | accepted | State novelty against the named prior work; scope mediation claims accordingly. | Authors |
| 12 | **No field or human study.** | The review-capacity work uses declared parameters, not observed reviewers. Automation bias means human approval is a weaker safeguard than assumed (Skitka 1999; Goddard 2012). No claim about real reviewer behaviour is supported. | open (accepted as out of scope) | A field or human-subjects study of reviewer behaviour and approval quality. Close only with observed-reviewer data. | Human-factors / research owner |

## Outstanding qualification gates

The paper's Table 4 gap-closure obligations remain outstanding. This third audit
fixed E1, E2, R1, and R2 and added an offline adaptive attacker, but it does not
discharge Table 4: the deployment obligations above (external key custody,
assurance of the trusted base, durable outbox, adaptive-attacker evaluation, and a
human study) are the conditions under which the corresponding claims may be
relied upon. Until those gates are met, the guarantees hold only under the stated
trust assumptions and only for the proposals the offline suite and the bounded
adaptive attacker exercise.
